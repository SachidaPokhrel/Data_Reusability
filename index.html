Week 12 · Genomic data re-use · Completed pilot

Conserved genes, variable neighborhoods?

A pilot reanalysis of the Peronospora effusa pangenome asks whether coding conservation extends upstream. Its only screening hit revealed a boundary problem: the supposedly comparable windows covered different parts of a nearly identical locus.

01 / Research question

Does conservation extend beyond the coding sequence?

A gene can be shared across isolates without having an identical coding sequence. Even a highly conserved CDS may sit next to variable noncoding DNA. We reused a published pangenome to test that distinction at single-copy core loci and to ask whether fixed windows anchored to gene annotations give a reliable comparison.

The original study used its assemblies and graph to investigate genomic variation and resistance breaking. Here, the published resources serve a different purpose: checking whether conservation of a gene also describes conservation of its upstream neighborhood. Upstream windows are candidate regulatory regions; their sequence does not establish promoter activity or gene expression.

02 / Data and quality control

Define core across 19; compare sequences in six

We downloaded and checksum-verified the published assemblies, gene and repeat annotations, and orthogroup tables. Each populated haplotype call was resolved through its GFF3 transcript parent. The strict gene-level set required both calls to agree within each isolate, representation in all 19 isolates, and no assignment of the same isolate–gene pair to another orthogroup. This counts assembly gene models; it is not a comparison of both phased genomes per isolate.

Annotation QC inspected 195,934 gene models and flagged 8,551 across the full annotation set. That model count is distinct from the 1,802 orthogroups removed by all-isolate CDS QC. The six pilot isolates were Pe2, Pe5, Pe14, Pe18, Pe9 and Pe8, selected by farthest-point sampling from the published genotype matrix. Pe6 remained in the all-19 core definition; its repeat annotation was absent from the deposit and it was not among the six pilot isolates.

We extracted strand-aware windows immediately upstream of the annotated gene boundary at 500 and 1,000 bp. A locus needed usable windows in all six isolates to proceed to upstream alignment. MAFFT aligned the CDS and upstream sequences separately. Comparisons used matched callable isolate pairs, with coverage reported separately from identity. The prespecified combined divergence cutoffs were ≤1% for conserved CDS, ≤1% for conserved upstream DNA, and ≥5% for variable upstream DNA. Intermediate upstream scores and technical failures were kept visible in class E.

04 / Locus validation

Chr5_38: the windows did not line up

The original 500 bp screen measured 0.37% CDS divergence and 42.35% combined upstream divergence. Mean upstream alignment coverage was 89.68%, and all 15 isolate pairs passed the matching filter. No annotated repeats overlapped the measured windows. Yet the pairwise results split into groups: Pe5 and Pe14 were similar to each other, and their windows were much less similar to those in the other isolates. High callability did not demonstrate that MAFFT had aligned equivalent positions.

All six genes are on the minus strand. The annotated 5′ boundary lay 45–57 bp upstream of the representative CDS start in Pe5 and Pe14, versus 1,120–1,221 bp in the other four isolates. We realigned the larger genomic intervals from the focal CDS anchor to the nearest upstream neighbor, including 60 bp of the focal CDS, and projected each original window onto that alignment.

Validation result. The original windows shared zero alignment columns in all eight between-group comparisons, while the larger intervals were 99.60–99.90% identical at callable bases. The screen compared different parts of a largely conserved locus.

05 / Interpretation

What the pilot established

The pilot demonstrated a failure mode of annotation-anchored windows: a shift in the annotated boundary can make a largely conserved locus appear highly variable. The original class-B call remains part of the screening record, and the follow-up is a separate validation result. No screen hit validated as divergence between comparable upstream windows in this pilot. Only one hit required that follow-up; unresolved loci were not ruled out.

This result does not tell us which boundary best reflects the true transcription start site, whether gene regulation differs, or whether repeats cause upstream variation. The repeat data support a coordinate-overlap description, but the available classifications do not support TE class or insertion history. In addition, the pilot isolates represent a diversity-selected subset of published assemblies, not a random species sample.

Next experiment, if the project continues

Test a small, prespecified set of loci using comparable alignment columns between verified anchors. Report how many loci become comparable, how many remain unresolved, and how the new calls differ from the original fixed-window screen. Do not start full mode until that design is validated. Neither original window passed the pilot gate.
